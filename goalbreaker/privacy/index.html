Privacy policy.

What the game uses, why it uses it, and the choices you have.

Effective date: 19 November 2026

What changed on 19 November 2026: version 1.3.0 adds an optional leaderboard (section 07) and, on iPhone, a check for a newer game version (section 05). We rewrote the Türkiye section, with a Turkish information notice (Aydınlatma Metni).

01Your progress stays local.

No cloud save. If you are 16 or over and join the optional leaderboard, it creates an anonymous ID and shows your nickname, kit and scores (section 07).

02Optional means opt-in.

Sending play statistics and crash reports is optional. Choose each separately in version 1.0.3 and later.

03Ad choices are separate.

Rewarded videos are optional. From version 1.0.6, a full-screen ad can also appear, at most once every five won levels. The ad service can process data when it preloads ads. On iOS, ads are personalised only if you allow tracking when the game asks. From version 1.1.0 on Android and version 1.2.1 on iOS, optional one-time purchases can remove ads; Google Play or Apple’s App Store handles the payment.

04The leaderboard is opt-in.

Players aged 16 and over can join with a nickname. Players aged 13 to 15 can only watch; under 13, there is no leaderboard. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland it is view-only. Leave and delete your leaderboard data in Settings.

01Who we are

This privacy policy covers the Goalbreaker mobile game and this website. Goalbreaker is developed and published by Doğukan Saçmaözü, operating as Talvero Games, in Türkiye (“we”, “us”). We are responsible for the personal data we process for the purposes described here.

For privacy questions or requests, contact dogukangamesdev@gmail.com. If you need a postal address for a written request, ask at the same address and we will send it.

02Your game, on your device

Goalbreaker does not require a player account. It stores your level progress, stars, coins, gems, unlocked items, trophies, daily rewards, preferences and the totals it counts for the leaderboard in the app’s local storage. We do not upload your save file to a game server or provide our own cloud-save service. If you are 16 or over and join the optional leaderboard, the game sends a nickname, your kit and your scores to our leaderboard database; section 07 explains what it sends and how to delete it.

Settings → Reset progress clears local game progress. It does not clear the leaderboard totals the game keeps on your device or your leaderboard identity, and it does not delete your leaderboard entries; use Settings → Leave the ranking and delete my data for that (section 07). Uninstalling the app normally removes its local storage, but your operating system may retain or restore app data through device backups. Backups are controlled by your device settings and platform provider. From version 1.0.4 on Android, the game limits Android backup and phone-to-phone transfer to its progress file: settings, your age range and the identifiers used by Firebase Analytics, Firebase Crashlytics and AdMob are not included. If you have joined the leaderboard, the progress file also holds the key to your leaderboard identity, so a backup or phone-to-phone transfer can bring that identity back on a restored or new device. On Android 9 and later the progress file goes to cloud backup only when that backup is end-to-end encrypted. On iOS, the game does not limit backups: iCloud and computer backups can include the progress file, the game’s settings (including your age range) and identifiers stored by the services below, so restoring a backup can bring them back. This includes your leaderboard identity if you have joined. Resetting or uninstalling the game does not delete data already sent to the services below, including your leaderboard entries. Delete your leaderboard data in the game before you uninstall it (section 07).

From version 1.0.4, the main screen of the game shows a link to our Instagram page (@goalbreakergame) to players aged 13 and over. Opening it hands you over to the Instagram app or your browser, where Instagram’s own privacy policy applies. The game receives no information from Instagram and cannot see whether you follow us. On Android, the one-time in-game reward for opening the page is kept with your local progress; the iPhone version gives no reward for opening the page.

03Optional play statistics & crash reports

Gameplay analytics and crash reporting are optional. On Android, from version 1.0.3, new installations ask you to choose at first launch, with both options initially off. On iPhone, the game does not ask at launch and both options stay off until you turn one on. On both platforms you can continue playing without enabling either option and change each choice at any time in Settings → Data & privacy. Advertising privacy choices are separate.

Play statistics · Firebase Analytics

Information can include level starts and results, shots, stars, time spent in an attempt, retries and continues, use and acquisition of powers, virtual-currency costs, screens viewed, rewards, cosmetic items unlocked and how they were obtained, attempts to open a locked chapter, when the game shows you the stars a locked chapter still needs and whether you open its list of levels to replay, which game mode you play and your answer if you reply to a one-time in-game question about whether you like a game mode, ad impressions, clicks and reward results reported by the ads SDK, session activity, app version, device and operating system information, language, an app-instance identifier, Google Play install referral information (Android only) and approximate location (such as city, region and country) derived from an IP address. We use it to understand how the game is played and improve difficulty and rewards. We do not add your leaderboard nickname or leaderboard ID, an email address or an account ID. Our Firebase Analytics configuration disables advertising ID collection on Android and, on both platforms, denies advertising storage, advertising user data and advertising personalisation for this analytics integration. On iOS, it also turns off collection of the identifier for vendor (IDFV).

Gameplay events generated before you enable analytics are not later uploaded by the game. Some values sent with later events come from progress stored on this device and can reflect play from before you turned analytics on, for example the attempt number for a level, how many uses of a power you hold, your daily-reward day and how many stars a chapter still needs. Turning analytics off stops the game’s event uploads, disables analytics collection and resets locally buffered Firebase Analytics data. It does not automatically erase information already received by the service.

Crash reports · Firebase Crashlytics

Reports can include crash traces and error details, app version, device model and operating system, memory and storage information, installation identifiers, and game context such as the level, language and graphics setting. While crash reporting is on, Crashlytics also records app session information (Firebase Sessions), and when analytics is on as well, a crash report can include a short trail of the gameplay events recorded before it. We use this information to diagnose and fix technical problems.

Crashlytics can retain crash information on the device while reporting is disabled. If you later enable reporting, locally held crash reports may be sent. Turning reporting off stops the game’s own custom crash logs immediately; the SDK’s automatic reporting opt-out takes full effect on the next app launch. Restart the game after disabling this option.

These identifiers distinguish installations; this information is not necessarily anonymous. Turning sharing off does not automatically erase information already sent; the retention and privacy-rights sections below still apply.

Versions 1.0.2 and earlier use a combined Share usage data switch for analytics and crash reporting. When upgrading, an existing explicit choice is preserved for both options; you can then change them independently. Resetting game progress does not reset these privacy choices.

Test versions 1.0.0 to 1.0.3 also sent crash, ANR (app not responding) and performance diagnostics together with an installation identifier to Unity Technologies through Unity’s Engine Diagnostics service, regardless of the in-game choices. Later versions do not include this service.

04Ads & advertising privacy

The game uses Google AdMob for two kinds of ads. Rewarded videos are optional: one plays only when you choose it for an extra boost or an in-game reward. From version 1.0.6, the game can also show a full-screen ad (an interstitial) when you leave the results screen of a level you have won. This happens at most once every five won levels and only if at least two minutes have passed since the last full-screen ad, including a rewarded video, closed. Only won levels count, including replays of a level; losing a level, using Retry after a loss or watching a rewarded video does not. Interstitials are not shown during play, when the game opens or after a lost level, and if no ad is ready you continue straight away. Versions 1.0.5 and earlier show rewarded videos only.

Once the advertising privacy flow allows ad requests, the ad service can initialise and preload a rewarded video and, from version 1.0.6, an interstitial before any ad is shown, even when no ad is due.

Google and its advertising partners may process IP addresses and approximate location, device and app information, app launches and ad interactions, the ads you have seen, performance diagnostics, and device identifiers. On Android these include the Android advertising ID where available and, where applicable, identifiers related to accounts signed in on the device; the SDK also uses app set ID for permitted purposes such as analytics and fraud prevention. On iOS they can include identifiers limited to this app or its developer. Advertising data is used to deliver and measure ads, maintain the service and prevent fraud, with personalisation depending on applicable consent and settings. The game does not request precise GPS location.

AdMob can also offer an ad request to other advertising companies that bid for it through Google’s mediation. Google runs the auction. Each company that takes part receives information from the ad request, such as IP address, device and app information and, where permitted, device identifiers, to decide whether to bid; the company whose ad is shown also receives what it needs to deliver and measure that ad. These companies use this information under their own privacy policies, and the list of companies can change. Where a regional advertising privacy message is shown, it lists the advertising partners. Google states that ad requests with child-directed treatment, which the game requests for every player under 16, are not sent to these bidders.

Google’s User Messaging Platform manages regional advertising privacy messages, including for the European Economic Area, United Kingdom, Switzerland and supported US states. The message and options available depend on your region and the service’s requirements. Where required, Settings → Ad privacy options lets you review your choices. You may also use your device’s advertising controls.

On iOS, the game asks players aged 16 or over once for Apple’s App Tracking Transparency permission, after any regional advertising message. Only if you allow tracking can Google and its advertising partners use your device’s advertising identifier (IDFA) and show personalised ads, subject to your other advertising choices. If you do not allow it, or are not asked (players under 16, or after refusing every purpose in the European advertising message), every ad request from the game asks for non-personalised ads, whatever you choose in other privacy options, and the IDFA is not available. You can change your answer at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

From version 1.0.4, every ad request asks for content suitable for general audiences only. The game requests no ads and shows no regional advertising message until the age question below is answered. For players under 16, ads are requested with Google’s child treatment: no personalised ads or remarketing, no third-party ad vendors and no advertising ID, and the regional advertising message is not shown.

To report an inappropriate ad, tap the ad’s info or AdChoices icon and choose to report it, or email us at dogukangamesdev@gmail.com with the time and a short description of the ad.

Turning off gameplay analytics or crash reporting does not turn off AdMob processing. Read how Google uses information from apps that use its services and Google’s advertising privacy information for more about these services and controls.

05App updates

From version 1.0.3 on Android, the game can check Google Play for an eligible update and offer Google’s optional in-app update flow. Play handles availability, downloads and installation. Downloading begins only after your confirmation; installation and restarting require a further choice. You can keep playing or decline an update. Your device’s separate automatic-update settings remain controlled by Google Play. On iOS, updates are installed through the App Store.

Update requests use device metadata, the application version and the list of installed app modules and asset packs to determine update availability and expected download size. This store functionality is separate from optional Firebase gameplay analytics and advertising choices. See Google’s in-app update data-safety information.

From version 1.3.0, the iOS version asks Apple’s public App Store lookup service whether a newer version of the game exists, after the launch privacy steps (the age question and, where they apply, the advertising message and Apple’s tracking prompt). The request names only the game; it carries no identifier of you or your device. From the answer, the game uses only the version number and the address of the game’s App Store page. As with any internet request, Apple receives your IP address, under its own privacy policy. If a newer version exists, the game can show a panel that opens the App Store page. The panel is optional: you can dismiss it, and LATER keeps it from showing again for that version for 3 days. Updating stays in the App Store.

06Purchases

From version 1.1.0 on Android and version 1.2.1 on iOS, the game offers optional one-time purchases that remove ads: No Ads Between Levels, Remove All Ads (with a lower-priced upgrade for owners of No Ads Between Levels or the Starter Pack) and the Starter Pack (no ads between levels, plus coins and gems). None of them is a subscription. Google Play or Apple’s App Store handles the payment under its own terms and privacy policy; we never receive your card or bank details.

On Android, Google Play tells the game which of these products your Google account owns, with a purchase token and an order number. The game checks the purchase’s signature on your device and confirms the purchase to Google Play. If you reinstall the game, or play on another Android device with the same Google account, Google Play restores your purchases.

On iOS, the game uses Apple’s StoreKit 2. The App Store on your device tells the game which of these products your Apple Account owns; the game counts only purchases that StoreKit has verified as signed by Apple and not refunded, and confirms them to the App Store. To do so it handles the product identifier and a shortened, one-way code made from the transaction number; it saves which products you own, not transaction numbers. Restore purchases asks the App Store to check your Apple Account again, which may ask you to sign in. If Ask to Buy is on for your account, a purchase waits until a parent or guardian approves it. These purchases are not shared through Family Sharing.

On both platforms, the game keeps the ownership with your local progress so it also works offline. We run no purchase server of our own. Nothing about your purchases or your coins is sent to the leaderboard. Purchases belong to the store account you bought them with and do not move between Android and iOS. A refunded or cancelled purchase can be removed from the game.

Google Play gives us order reports with details such as the order number, product, price, date and the buyer’s location (country, and in some reports state or postal code). Apple gives us sales and financial reports with details such as the product, price, date and the buyer’s country or region, without your name or Apple Account. We use these reports for accounting, tax obligations, refunds and support. If you have turned on play statistics, Firebase Analytics also records a completed Google Play purchase automatically, with the product, its price and currency. On iOS, Firebase states that it records purchases automatically only for Apple’s original purchase interface, not for StoreKit 2, which the game uses. The game sends no purchase events of its own on either platform, and nothing about purchases goes to Firebase while play statistics are off.

After Remove All Ads, the game no longer starts the ad service, so the processing described in the advertising section stops for new ad requests (in the session of the purchase the service may already be running); on iOS it then does not ask for tracking permission either. With No Ads Between Levels or the Starter Pack, optional rewarded videos remain, and the advertising section still applies.

07Leaderboard

From version 1.3.0, Goalbreaker has an optional leaderboard, shown in the game as RANKING, with weekly and all-time tables for stars, levels and trophies (trophy points). It is the game’s only feature that sends information about you to a database we run. Nothing about you is sent to it unless you are 16 or over, are outside the regions named below, and choose to join.

Who can join

  • Players who answered the game’s age question with 16 or over can join from the ranking screen. The game keeps only your age range on your device and does not send your age. If you are 16 or 17, we suggest you talk to a parent or guardian before joining.
  • Players aged 13 to 15 can view the tables but cannot join. For them the game sends no leaderboard ID, nickname or score, and shows only nicknames we have reviewed; other names appear as “Player”. They can report a nickname by email.
  • If you answered that you are under 13, the leaderboard does not exist for you: the game does not show it and sends no leaderboard request of any kind.
  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the leaderboard is view-only: you cannot join, no leaderboard identity is created, the game sends nothing about you, and nicknames are not shown (each row reads “Player” with a four-digit number, such as “Player 1234”, which your device derives only for display; it does not identify the player, and no player names or IDs are shown there). The game decides this on your device from device signals only: on Android, the country of the mobile network, then the country of the SIM card, then the device’s region setting; on iPhone, the device’s region setting. If any available signal names one of these countries, or no signal is available, the leaderboard is view-only. The game reads these signals on your device, keeps them in memory only, and never stores or sends them. If we open the leaderboard in these regions later, we will update this policy first.

Before you can type a nickname, the game shows a summary of the leaderboard rules with links to the full leaderboard rules and to this section. You join by ticking “I accept the ranking rules”, a box that starts unticked. Ticking it accepts the rules of the leaderboard, the terms on which we run it for you; it is not a consent to this policy.

What the leaderboard stores, and who can read it

When you join, the game creates an anonymous leaderboard identity with Google’s Firebase Authentication: a random ID. It does not ask for an email address, password or phone number, and it does not use your device’s advertising ID or other device identifiers. The game keeps the key to this identity in its progress file on your device; device backups carry it, so restoring a backup can bring the identity back (section 02).

For each player who joins, the leaderboard stores:

  • the nickname you choose, a simplified copy of it used for filtering and search, and whether we have reviewed it;
  • the ID of the kit your player wears;
  • your weekly and all-time scores for stars, levels and trophy points, and, behind the trophy points, the six weekly trophy steps of the current week and how many bronze, silver and gold collection trophies you hold;
  • when you joined, when each score last rose (used to break ties), when your game last sent scores, and when you last changed your nickname;
  • the version number of the game and the version of the ranking rules you accepted;
  • your random ID, which is also the name of your entry in the database;
  • a check value: a one-way code (a hash) computed from your random ID and a secret that only your device shows, taken together, so that one guess cannot unlock every player’s code. It lets us check that an emailed deletion request comes from you. The game never sends the secret itself; it only shows it to you as part of your ranking code.

Everything in this list is public. Other players see your nickname, kit, scores and rank in the game; the weekly winner of each table shows a crown for one week. Anyone who reads the leaderboard database directly, outside the game, can read every item in the list. Do not put personal information, such as your full name, phone number, address or social media handle, in your nickname.

Not public: reports (who reported whom), your daily report counter, our moderation records and our moderation and deletion log. Only the developer can read them.

We never collect for the leaderboard your real name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, location, advertising ID or purchase information. No coin balance, earned or bought, is sent to it.

The game counts your scores on your device from your progress. It sends them after you leave the results of a won level (at most about once a minute), when you open the ranking screen, and when the game starts with an update it could not send earlier. Your save file itself is not uploaded.

Where it is stored

Leaderboard entries, reports and moderation records are stored in Google Cloud Firestore in the game’s Firebase project, in Frankfurt, Germany. Your leaderboard identity (the random ID and the key that lets your game use it) is held by Firebase Authentication, which Google runs from data centres in the United States. Google acts as our service provider (data processor) for both, under its data processing terms, which include the EU standard contractual clauses and the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework. We read moderation data from Türkiye.

To answer the game’s requests, Google receives your device’s IP address and basic device and connection information, such as the app’s user-agent string. This also happens when a player who has not joined opens the tables. Firebase Authentication keeps logged IP addresses for a few weeks. We do not store IP addresses in the leaderboard database, and we keep the database’s access logs, which would record them, turned off. When we or the game delete leaderboard data, Google removes it from its live systems and finishes removing it from its backup systems within 180 days.

Why we use it

We use this information to run the tables and ranks, to break ties, to give the weekly rewards, to keep scores fair, to moderate nicknames, to handle reports and to answer requests. We do not use leaderboard information for advertising or analytics: we do not send it to AdMob or advertising partners, and the game never passes your leaderboard ID, its key or your nickname to Firebase Analytics, Crashlytics or the ad service.

Nicknames and moderation

When you set a nickname, the game and the database check it automatically against character rules and a list of blocked words; a nickname that passes appears straight away. You can change your nickname once a day. Every other moderation decision is made by a person, the developer, on a moderation page that only the developer can open: renaming a nickname and locking it, removing a player from a week’s tables, and banning a leaderboard identity. A banned identity cannot send scores and receives no leaderboard rewards. When we act on your entry, the game tells you what was done, which rule it broke (offensive or inappropriate name; pretending to be someone else; cheating or impossible score; something else), that a person decided, and how to contest the decision by email. We keep no free-text notes about players.

Reporting and hiding

If you have joined, you can report a player from the ranking screen; a report covers the player, not only the nickname. A report holds your random ID, the reported player’s random ID, the week, the reason you chose and the time. A daily counter holds how many reports you sent that day (at most 10) and the last player you reported. The reported player is not told who reported them. Players who cannot report in the game, for example players aged 13 to 15 or players who have not joined, can email dogukangamesdev@gmail.com; the game opens a draft with the table, rank, nickname and date and adds no ID. We aim to review reports within 24 hours; when we cannot review them for a longer time, we pause the leaderboard. Hiding a player is stored only on your device and is not sent to us; you can show hidden players again in Settings.

Rewards

Each week the top 10 of each of the three weekly tables (stars, levels and trophies) can collect coins and gems in the game during the reward period (see the leaderboard rules). Taking part is free, and rewards have no cash value and cannot be exchanged for money. To pay rewards, the game reads the top of each weekly table; it records collected rewards in your progress file on your device.

Leaving and deleting your leaderboard data

Use Settings → Leave the ranking and delete my data (the same control is also in the help (?) sheet of the ranking screen). It is there whenever your game holds a leaderboard identity, whatever your age range, and also while the leaderboard is paused. Leaving always deletes your leaderboard data; there is no way to leave and keep your entries.

Before anything is deleted, the game asks you to confirm and tells you what is removed. If you confirm, your entries leave every table at once: weekly entries whose reward period is over are deleted, and your other entries become empty markers that hold only your random ID and the time of deletion. The game then deletes your leaderboard identity from Firebase Authentication. If you are offline or the game closes first, it keeps trying by itself and tells you when the deletion has finished. If Google does not allow the empty identity to be deleted at that moment, the game forgets it and we delete it within 30 days. In the rare case that the game cannot finish, it tells you so and asks you to email us your ranking code, and we finish the deletion.

If the age answer stored on your device is below 16 and the game still holds a leaderboard identity (for example after a backup is restored and the age question is answered again), the game deletes that identity’s leaderboard data by itself, in the same way, after the launch privacy steps. Players aged 13 to 15 are told once; for players under 13 the game shows nothing.

What stays after a deletion, and for how long:

  • the empty markers, which keep a deleted entry from coming back: removed within 30 days, together with the reports you filed and your report counter;
  • reports other players filed about you: deleted when we close them, at the latest after 90 days; a deleted identity can no longer file reports, and where another player’s daily report counter named you, that entry is blanked;
  • if your identity was banned, the ban record, while the ban applies;
  • a line in our moderation and deletion log (a short code, the action, a reason code and the date): 12 months;
  • Google’s backups: up to 180 days, as described above.

Unclaimed rewards are lost, and your game progress stays. If you join again later, you start with a new identity, and your scores are sent again from your device.

Ranking code

While your game holds a leaderboard identity, Settings shows your ranking code under the delete control. It has two parts: a short part that lets us find your entries, and a secret part that only your device shows. Tap the code to copy it. Do not share it with anyone but us.

If your game loses a leaderboard identity, or its entries could not be removed, the game keeps that identity’s full ranking code (up to three) on your device under Settings → Earlier ranking codes, so you can still ask us by email to delete that data. You can clear these codes there; this removes them from your device only.

To have your leaderboard data deleted without the game, for example after uninstalling it, email dogukangamesdev@gmail.com with the subject “Goalbreaker privacy request” and your whole ranking code. The secret part shows that the request comes from you; we then delete your data as described above. We may ask for more information to confirm a request, and we answer within 30 days. If you no longer have the code, for example after reinstalling without a backup, write to us with your nickname. If we cannot confirm that the entries are yours, we replace the nickname with a neutral one and do not delete the scores, because we will not delete someone’s scores at another person’s request. The leaderboard rules repeat these steps.

Retention

  • Weekly entries: kept for the week and the 14-day reward period after it, then deleted, at the latest 45 days after the week ends.
  • Your all-time entry and your leaderboard identity: until you delete them, or ask us to; an all-time entry that receives no new score for 24 months is deleted, and the anonymous sign-in identity behind it is deleted as well.
  • After a deletion: as listed in the box above.
  • Reports: deleted when we close them, at the latest after 90 days. Report counters: 7 days.
  • Our moderation and deletion log: 12 months. A ban: while it applies.
  • Support emails, including leaderboard requests: 12 months after the request is closed.

We do not promise to restore leaderboard entries or scores that are lost.

Contact

Questions about the leaderboard, its rules or your data: dogukangamesdev@gmail.com. The help button (?) on the ranking screen shows this address too, with links to the leaderboard rules and the help page.

08When you contact us

If you email us, we receive your email address, the name shown by your email service, your message and any attachments you choose to send. We use them to respond, investigate the issue and handle your request. Support email is handled through Gmail. The game does not send support messages automatically.

Please send only what is needed to explain the issue. We do not need your passwords, payment details or identity documents for an ordinary support request. For a question about a purchase, the order number from your Google Play receipt (it starts with “GPA.”) helps us find it; for an App Store purchase, the product and the date are enough. We never need your card details.

10Retention & security

Different information has different retention periods:

  • Local game data: stays on your device until removed, subject to device backup behaviour; Reset progress keeps the leaderboard totals and identity (section 07).
  • Crash reports: Google states that Crashlytics keeps crash traces and associated identifiers for 90 days before beginning removal from live and backup systems.
  • Analytics: user-level data follows the Analytics property’s configured retention period. Aggregated reports may remain longer. Turning sharing off does not itself delete past reports.
  • Advertising data: retention depends on Google and the relevant advertising partner, the purpose and applicable requirements.
  • Purchase records: Google Play and Apple keep your order history under their own policies. We keep the order, sales and financial reports we receive as long as tax and accounting law requires (in Türkiye, generally five years) and as needed for refunds and support.
  • Leaderboard: weekly entries are deleted after the week’s 14-day reward period, at the latest 45 days after the week ends; your all-time entry and identity stay until you delete them, and an all-time entry that receives no new score for 24 months is deleted together with the anonymous sign-in identity behind it; after a deletion, empty markers and the identity are removed within 30 days; reports are deleted when closed and at the latest after 90 days; report counters after 7 days; our moderation and deletion log after 12 months; a ban stays while it applies. Google keeps logged sign-in IP addresses for a few weeks and removes deleted data from its backups within 180 days. Section 07 has the details.
  • Support messages: we keep messages for 12 months after your request is closed, to answer follow-up questions and show how a request was handled, unless a legal obligation or a claim requires longer.

We use access controls to protect the services we manage. Only the developer can access the game’s Firebase project, with a Google account protected by 2-step verification.
